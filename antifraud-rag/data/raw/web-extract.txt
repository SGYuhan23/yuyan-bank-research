L28: 
L29: cite29†Image: A man is looking worried with his hand on his forehead  L30: John worked as an accounts manager for a local manufacturing business. Late on a Friday afternoon, he received an email which appeared to be from one of his regular suppliers, Mr Liu from Zhang-Fei Industries, a ball bearings supplier in Asia. Mr Liu’s email explained that due to a change in their internal finance system, he needed John to update their banking details, including a new account number.
L31: If you receive an email with a request involving financial details that you know nothing about, don’t action it until you are sure it’s the real deal.
L32: 
L33: John took the email at face value and changed the banking information in his company’s database. A few days later, John made a scheduled payment to Zhang-Fei Industries for $17,000.
L34: Two weeks later the ball bearings from Zheng Fei industries had not arrived so John telephoned Mr Liu. Mr Liu said he hadn’t received payment for the last order and had consequently cancelled shipment. John told Mr Liu that he had processed the payment personally to make sure it was paid according to the new arrangements. After some investigation it became clear that Mr Liu had not sent any request to update his company’s banking details and John was the victim of a scam.
L35: In the weeks to come, with the initial loss of $17,000, the delay in supply flowing from missed orders and broken contractual obligations, John’s company estimated their loss to be over $30,000.
L36: 
L37: *The story above is based on one or more real scam reports received by the ACCC. For privacy purposes the names and images of victims have not been used.

L347: 
L348: Last updated: 4 September 2026
L349: 
L350: Before you invest your money, take time to check who you’re dealing with and what you’re investing in.
L351: ## Your quick investment scam checklist
L352: 
L353: According to the National Anti-Scam Centre (NASC), Australians reported losing more than $837 million to investment scams in 2025. This checklist can help protect you.
L354: 
L355: STOP
L356: 
L357: Before you invest, stop and ask yourself:
L358:   * Am I being pressured to invest quickly?
L359:   * Does the investment promise high returns with little or no risk?
L360:   * Am I relying on testimonials, celebrity endorsements, social media posts or online reviews?
L361:   * Have I taken time to research the investment independently?
L362: 
L363: CHECK
L364: 
L365: Before you invest, check:
L366:   * The company is cite345†licensed by ASIC†www.asic.gov.au to advise on or offer the investment.
L367:   * The company is not fake or impersonating a real company or licensee.
L368:   * You have independently verified the company’s contact details.
L369:   * You understand how the investment works – you can explain how the investment makes money and where the returns come from.
L370:   * You have read the offer documents.
L371:   * If it might be an cite285†investment scam or a cite278†scam website .
L372:   * Recent scam warnings from cite346†ASIC’s scams hub†www.asic.gov.au and recent alerts from the MoneySmart cite292†Investor Alert List .
L373: PROTECT
L374: 
L375: Protect yourself by making sure you know:
L376: 
L377:   * What to do if cite289†something goes wrong .
L378:   * How to cite317†make a complaint about the financial product or service.
L379:   * The warning signs of investment scams and investment scam journeys.
L380:   * How to cite290†report a scam .
L381: ## STOP: Watch out for investment scams
L382: 
L383: Investment scams are serious crimes and scammers are sophisticated. They use professional-looking websites, social media, advertising and AI technology to make scams appear genuine.
L384: 
L385: If an offer sounds too good to be true – like easy money or guaranteed returns – stop and step back.
L386: 
L387: Understanding how investment scams work can help you avoid losing money. It’s important to know that anyone can be a victim.
L388: Watch out for these red flags, if the person or company offering the investment:
L389: 
L390:   * does not have an Australian financial services (AFS) license or says they don't need one
L391:   * constantly contacts you (phone calls, texts or emails) and pressures you to make a quick decision
L392:   * uses the name of a reputable organisation to gain credibility
L393:   * has an investment prospectus that isn't cite347†registered with ASIC†www.asic.gov.au L394:   * offers you very high investment returns
L395: If you spot any of these signs, hang up the phone or delete the email. If you manage to record any of the scammer's details, document these to note when you report the scam.
L396:

L398: Artificial intelligence (AI) is super-charging online scams. In the 2025/26 financial year, ASIC removed more than 19,400 online scams – up 182% on the previous year – including fake websites, social media ads, phishing scams and cryptocurrency investment scams. cite346†Learn more about ASIC's work†www.asic.gov.au .

L401: ## CHECK: Is the company/person real and licensed or authorised
L402: 
L403: In Australia, companies or people offering investments must have an Australian Financial Services cite348†(AFS) licence or be authorised by someone who does.
L404: 
L405: An AFS licence:
L406: 
L407:   * controls what kinds of financial products they can sell
L408:   * gives better protection to investors if things go wrong
L409: It's important to know that holding an AFS licence doesn't mean that ASIC endorses the company, financial product, or advice. It doesn't guarantee the investment is safe or that you won’t lose money from dealing with them.
L410: 
L411: With a proliferation of cite278†AI-generated scam websites , online reviews, videos, and social media posts, it’s also important to not rely on contact details, links or phone numbers provided in an investment offer, text message, email, social media post or online advertisement.
L412: Scammers misuse AFS licence details by claiming to hold a licence, using another entity’s licence number, or impersonating a licensed financial services business.
L413: 
L414: Before investing, make sure you verify the licence holder’s name and number on cite345†ASIC’s professional registers†www.asic.gov.au , and ensure those details match the business or investment opportunity being promoted. Relying solely on advertisements, websites or online search results can put consumers at risk.
